A demand letter is the last cheap step in a dispute. It costs a stamp and an hour, it occasionally resolves the matter outright, and when it does not, it becomes one of the most useful documents in the eventual case file: a dated, unemotional statement of what happened and what you asked for, written before anyone was in litigation mode.

It is also frequently written badly. The two failure modes are opposite and equally damaging - a letter so vague that the recipient genuinely does not know what is being asked, or a letter so aggressive that it reads as a threat and makes settlement harder. This guide covers the structure that avoids both, and the delivery methods that let you prove the letter was sent.

## What a demand letter is actually for

A demand letter serves three purposes at once, and a good one does all three without effort.

First, it asks. Disputes sometimes persist because nobody has clearly stated what would resolve them. A precise figure and a deadline can end a matter that six months of frustrated phone calls did not.

Second, it records. It fixes in writing what happened, when, and what was requested - dated before any claim was filed, which makes it far more persuasive than a reconstruction written afterwards.

Third, it demonstrates reasonableness. Courts in general respond well to a party who made a clear, proportionate request before involving the system. Some jurisdictions expect it explicitly; even where they do not, it costs nothing to be the person who tried.

## The structure that works

A demand letter should fit on one or two pages. Length is not persuasion; specificity is.

### 1. Identify the parties and the subject

Open with your full name and address, the recipient's full legal name and address, the date, and one line identifying the matter - an invoice number, a contract date, an order reference, or the address of the property. If the recipient is a business, address it to the registered legal name and, where possible, a named person.

### 2. State the facts as a chronology

Three to six short paragraphs, or a dated list. Each entry states what happened and when, without characterisation. Compare these two versions of the same fact:

> On 14 March the deposit of $1,200 was paid by bank transfer. The work was scheduled to begin on 1 April. No work had begun by 30 April.

> They took my money in March and then completely ghosted me for months, which is obviously unacceptable.

The first is checkable and reads as reliable. The second asks the reader to take your side before they know anything.

### 3. State what was agreed

Identify the obligation you say was not met, and where it comes from - a signed contract clause, a written quote, an emailed confirmation, or the terms discussed in a message thread. If the agreement was oral, say so plainly and point to the documents that reference it.

### 4. State the amount and show the arithmetic

This is where most letters weaken. One figure, broken down, sourced to documents:

| Item | Amount | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deposit paid | $1,200.00 | Bank transfer 14 March, ref 88213 |
| Replacement contractor | $1,850.00 | Invoice #4471, 12 May |
| Less value of work completed | -$300.00 | Agreed estimate, email 3 May |
| **Total requested** | **$2,750.00** | |

A recipient who can see the arithmetic can dispute a line or pay it. A recipient who is given a round number with no derivation usually does neither.

<Callout type="warning" title="Common filing mistake">
Inflating the figure to leave negotiating room. If a claim is filed later, the amount on the claim form should match your evidence, and a demand letter asking for a different, higher number becomes an exhibit that undermines the figure you are asking a court to award.
</Callout>

### 5. State what you want and by when

One sentence for the remedy - payment, repair, replacement, refund - and one specific date. "Within 14 days" invites an argument about when the clock started. "By 5 September 2026" does not.

### 6. State what happens next, factually

A single sentence is enough: that if the matter is not resolved by the date given, you intend to pursue the claim through the appropriate court. Do not embellish this. Threats of criminal referral, contacting an employer, publicising the dispute, or anything beyond an ordinary civil remedy can transform a routine letter into a serious problem for the person who wrote it.

### 7. Close with contact details and an attachment list

Give a phone number and email, list the enclosures, and sign it.

## Sending it so you can prove you sent it

A demand letter that cannot be proved to have been sent is worth much less than one that can.

**Tracked or certified post** produces the strongest record, particularly where a signature is captured. Keep the receipt and print the tracking history when it shows delivery - tracking data is not retained online indefinitely.

**Email the same letter the same day.** It is instant, timestamped, and often the address the recipient actually monitors. Attach the letter as a PDF rather than pasting it into the body, so the document you send is the document you can later produce.

**Send to the right address.** For a business, the registered agent address is a defensible choice alongside the trading address. If a contract specifies where notices must be sent, use that address - a notice clause that is ignored can make the notice ineffective.

**Do not send only by text message or social media.** They are informal, easily lost, and rarely produce a record in a form that is convenient to present later.

<Callout type="tip">
Scan the signed letter and the postal receipt into the same PDF as soon as you send it, and name the file with the date. Six months later, the single most annoying gap in a case file is a demand letter everyone remembers sending and nobody can produce.
</Callout>

## After it is sent

Log the date sent, the method, the tracking reference and the deadline in your case timeline. Then wait for the deadline to pass before doing anything else. Chasing three days later undercuts the deadline you set.

If a reply arrives, keep it exactly as received. A partial admission, an offer, or an explanation that contradicts what was said earlier is all useful, and it is only useful in its original form. If the reply proposes settlement, get any agreement in writing and be clear about whether payment resolves the matter in full.

If the deadline passes with no response, the letter has done its remaining job: your file now contains a clear request, a delivery record, and silence.

## When to consult a lawyer

A demand letter is a procedural step, and this guide covers how one is put together. It does not cover whether your claim is legally sound, what a claim might be worth, whether a limitation period is running out, or how to respond to a counter-demand.

Talk to a licensed attorney in your jurisdiction before sending if the amount is significant, if a contract has a notice or dispute-resolution clause, if the other side is already represented, if the dispute involves injury or employment, or if a deadline may be close. A letter sent without understanding those issues can narrow options that were open beforehand.
